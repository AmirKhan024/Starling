1How Starling works

Much more than object detection

Spotting a person is only step one. Starling then places, recognises, shares, agrees and reasons, with no central computer.

  1. 1See people Ordinary systems stop here
  2. 2Place on the floor
  3. 3Recognise who
  4. 4Sign and share a note
  5. 5All cameras agree
  6. 6Reason about what is unseen

Engineers call these steps detection, floor mapping, re-identification, signed claims, a CRDT merge and negative evidence.

2Why it keeps working

No boss computer

Every camera thinks for itself. When one breaks, the rest carry on, because there is no server to fail.

3Reasoning

Finding someone no camera can see

Each working camera signs "nobody here". Rule out all those areas, and only one place is left.

A broken or covered camera stays silent, and silence is never taken as "nobody here". Engineers call this negative evidence.

4Trust

Catching a lying camera

A camera that reports someone where other cameras have signed "nobody here" is caught, and its trust drops.

Notes that break physics, such as walking through walls or teleporting, are caught the same way. Engineers call this Byzantine fault tolerance with reputation.

5Resilience

Cut the network, nothing breaks

Split the network and both halves keep working. Reconnect it and they agree again, by themselves.

Measured: after a split, every copy matched again about 2.5 s after the link came back. Engineers call this partition tolerance.

7Privacy

Privacy built in

Every question must state its purpose. Safety questions are answered; watching how hard people work is refused.

The purpose is signed, and each camera checks it before it answers.
